---
title: Spaced Repetition
tags: [learning, memory]
source: "[[How We Learn — Notes on Effective Studying]]"
created: 2026-06-22
---
Reviewing material across increasing intervals — a day later, then a few days,
then a week, then a month — instead of cramming it all into one session. Each
review is timed for roughly when I'm about to forget, so retrieval stays
effortful and the memory consolidates. Tools like Anki schedule these intervals
automatically. It's the temporal counterpart to active recall: spacing handles
*when* I study, and it pays off most when each spaced review is itself a
retrieval attempt rather than a reread.
